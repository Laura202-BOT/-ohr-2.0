Ohr
Passive Behavioral Monitoring for Bipolar Disorder
Ohr is a clinical decision-support system that passively monitors behavioral signals in bipolar disorder patients and delivers a Pre-Session Behavioral Summary to psychiatrists before each appointment — surfacing what happens between visits without requiring anything from the patient.

The Problem
Psychiatrists see bipolar patients for approximately one hour every few weeks. Everything in between is invisible. Patient self-report fails exactly when it matters most — during symptomatic periods when recall and insight are most impaired. Medication non-adherence affects approximately 50% of bipolar patients, yet clinicians have no reliable way to detect this between visits.
The Solution
Ohr passively tracks behavioral signals — sleep patterns, phone activity, movement, and social rhythm — and generates two core outputs delivered the night before each appointment:

Pre-Session Behavioral Summary — a one-page structured report translating passive behavioral data into clinically meaningful trend analysis relative to each patient's individual baseline
Behavioral Alert — flags significant deviations from a patient's normal behavioral range, telling the clinician to take a closer look

Clinical Validation
The behavioral signal framework was validated in structured discovery sessions with two senior psychiatrists at Rutgers University Hospital Department of Psychiatry:

Dr. Alexis Wirtz, DO — Chief Resident — confirmed clinical advisor
Dr. Najeeb Hussain — Vice-Chair of Clinical Affairs — clinical expert consultation

Both independently validated the core concept, the signal framework, and the one-page summary format.
Signal Framework
Clinically validated behavioral signals monitored by Ohr:

Sleep duration and quality
Medication adherence behavioral proxies
Food intake and eating regularity
Phone activity and late-night usage patterns
Mobility and movement
Social rhythm disruption
Racing thought proxies
Interpersonal conflict signals

Important
Ohr is a clinical decision-support tool only. It does not diagnose, prescribe, or replace clinical judgment. All signals are relative to each patient's individual baseline, not population averages.
